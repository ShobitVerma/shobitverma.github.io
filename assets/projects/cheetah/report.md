# A personal project in kinetic art

24-Bar Kinematic Mechanism with 1-DOF Dual-Phase Coupling for Gait Motion Synthesis

REPORT | Shobit Verma

Personal kinetic art project: cheetah form, linkage assembly and cyclic movement.

Project overview

This project explores the gallop of a cheetah as a moving mechanical sculpture. Six reference poses and their traced points guide a linkage design that turns one rotary input into coordinated leg movement. The mechanism uses a 24-bar arrangement with mirrored linkage sets, revolute joints and gear-driven phase coupling. The work includes path studies, CAD models, kinematic demonstrations and a first CNC-cut prototype.

The project combines gait observation, path synthesis, mechanical design and prototyping. Indian design application 519665-001 accompanies the report.

# 1. Artistic intent and design scope

The artistic aim is to make a running animal recognisable through motion as well as outline. A repeating cycle should express gathering beneath the body, reaching forward and returning through recovery. The cheetah silhouette, tail, layered plates and mechanically coordinated legs are developed as parts of the same kinetic composition.

Side view of the sculpture and mechanical linkage.

Single-input motion

The mechanism is driven by one input. Linkages distribute that motion to the legs and gears maintain the relationship between the mirrored sides. This reduces the number of independent drives required to produce the basic repeating gait.

Design development

# 5. Front- and rear-leg path synthesis

The reference paths guide the shape of the leg movement. A useful cyclic path must include forward reach, a lower part of the motion and recovery into the next step. The front- and rear-leg studies show the reference image combined with linkage geometry and traced curves.

# 6. Mechanism architecture

The described assembly consists of 24 bars connected through revolute joints and coupled through gears. Two similar linkage sets are mirrored and given a phase difference. Their combination produces coordinated motion while requiring only one independent input.

Side and end views of the complete assembly.

Plan view and geometric construction of the linkage arrangement.

The gear train is important to repeatability: it transfers the input motion and maintains the phase relationship. Joint placement and link lengths determine the path followed by each moving point.

# 7. Kinematic reasoning and phase coupling

Loop closure

For a planar closed linkage, the oriented link vectors around a loop sum to zero. In component form, the sum of l_i cos(theta_i) equals zero and the sum of l_i sin(theta_i) equals zero. Solving the loop constraints for successive input angles describes the linkage motion.

Mobility

The planar mobility relation M = 3(L − 1) − 2J₁ − J₂ can be used as a design check when the complete link and joint graph is available. The design uses one independent input; a full mobility analysis requires an inventory of every link, joint and gear constraint.

Coupler trajectories

A tracked point on a rotating link can be expressed as p(theta) = a(theta) + R(phi(theta))r, where a is a joint position, R is the link rotation and r is the point offset in link coordinates. Comparing p over one cycle with the reference path guides geometric refinement.

Phase relationship

The two mirrored sets are mechanically coupled with a phase difference. In an ideal phase model, theta_B = theta_A + delta. The phase offset sets the relative timing of the two linkage sets and must be selected together with the gear ratio and linkage geometry.

Practical interpretation

The demonstrated result is a kinetic mechanism and a moving prototype. Quantified running speed, ground-contact forces, payload, energy consumption and endurance have not been measured. Speed labels on the animal-reference screenshots refer to the animal reference footage and are not measured speeds of this mechanism.

Next steps

Further refinement can focus on smoother joint motion, reduced interference, repeatable phase setting and the visual balance of the sculpture. A measured trajectory comparison would help distinguish changes that improve path matching from changes made for artistic expression.
