A Robotic RCM-Based Needle Insertion System for Autonomous Abdominal Tumor Resection

Mechanical design at the RMML Lab, National Taiwan University.

01

Reorient the needle around a fixed entry point.

The starting design was bulky and could not change needle inclination during operation. It also lacked a direct way to feel insertion resistance.

I developed a compact mechanism with two independent motions: angular alignment about a remote center of motion (RCM), and needle insertion along its axis. The linkage sets the pivot mechanically; a releasable roller drive provides powered or manual insertion.

02

Principle of the design.

The needle needs to change its angle while continuing to pass through the same entry point. A double parallelogram creates that fixed pivot without placing a physical joint at the entry point.

Working of RCM.

The grounded link AC remains fixed while the paired parallelograms guide the needle along the virtual link EI, keeping its axis through the remote center O as the angle changes. Separate drives control angular alignment and needle insertion.

Kinematic structure.

The model shows the needle holder, paired linkage arms and two motor assemblies mounted beside the ultrasound probe. The linkage changes the needle angle, while a separate drive advances or retracts it through the holder.

Extreme positions.

The two views compare the home position with the opposite extreme of the mechanism. The linkage folds to lower the needle angle while maintaining the same entry point, showing the range of angular movement.

04

Governing equation for needle inclination from lead-screw position.

The original derivation maps lead-screw position LM to the needle inclination α. It defines the fixed geometry, constructs three triangle relations and evaluates the five equations in the order 1, 2, 5, 4 and 3.

Result of derivation

The inclination follows from θ1 + θ2 + α = 90°. The equations below retain the original source notation.

By Eq. 1
θ1=tan−1(CQML+MQ)
By Eq. 2
CL=ML+MQcosθ1
By Eq. 3
θ1+θ2+α=90°
By Eq. 4
θ3=β+(90°−θ2)
By Eq. 5
θ3=cos−1(KC2+CL2−KL22⁢KC⁢KL)

05

Compare the needle sweep with the required workspace.

With the RCM moved to the insertion point, the next question is how much of the target region the needle can reach. The workspace combines angular sweep about O with extension along the needle axis.

Define the target region.

The annotated ultrasound view supplies the required region and insertion point. It is the geometric reference for the study.

Compare the swept region.

The revised model overlays the needle sweep on that reference. The annotations show the reached region and the portions that fall outside the intended bounds.

06

Needle dispersion module.

After setting the direction, the needle advances along its axis through a geared roller transmission. The side handle engages the motor drive or releases it for free manual needle movement while the RCM linkage guides the axis.

Powered insertion

With the drive engaged, motor rotation is transmitted through the gears to the needle-deployment rollers. The latch retains the lever position.

The manual release is intended to allow direct tactile feedback and easier needle replacement.

07

Motion simulation.

The first study shows the linkage in motion. The revised study shows the same principle with the remote center located at the needle entry point. Together, they connect the design revision to the angular sweep shown in the workspace analysis.
