Monolithic Compliant Mechanism-Based Hand Grip Strength Analyzer

Mechanical design at the M2D2 Lab, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore.

01

Measure grip force through elastic deformation.

I developed a compliant hand grip strength analyser that uses the deformation of its structure to produce a mechanical indication of force. The design brings the grip interface, flexible arms and indicator into a compact mechanism, replacing conventional pin joints and separate springs with compliant geometry.

The work connects CAD design, beam stiffness, mechanical indication and finite element analysis. Changing the arm geometry provides a way to adapt sensitivity for different force ranges while keeping the same basic layout.

02

Principle of the design.

Grip force compresses the handle and bends the compliant arms. The resulting motion moves the indicator relative to the fixed frame, giving a visible displacement that can be mapped to force.

Working of the mechanism.

The frame provides a fixed reaction while the grip bridge transfers the applied load into the flexible arms. Their elastic bending moves the indicator, allowing the structure itself to provide the spring action.

Compliant structure.

The isometric view shows the thin flexural regions and the larger grip opening within the same part. The geometry connects the handle to the indicator without adding conventional revolute joints.

Load path.

The analysis view shows the applied handle load and the constrained mounting region. These conditions define how force enters the structure and where the frame reacts against it.

03

Tune stiffness through geometry.

The report uses cantilever beam theory to explain the force-to-deflection relationship. For a rectangular section, stiffness depends on the cube of thickness: increasing thickness raises stiffness, while a thinner arm gives more displacement under the same load.

Here, F is applied force, L is effective bending length, E is elastic modulus, I is the second moment of area, b is arm width and t is thickness. The calibration expression maps indicator displacement x to force; its coefficients must be obtained from known loads.

Grip strength analysis.

The report proposes force bands of 300 N for elderly users, 600 N for adults and 1200 N for high-strength users. The design challenge is to retain a readable indicator movement at lower grip forces while providing enough stiffness and structural margin for higher forces. A more compliant arm increases sensitivity in the lower range; a thicker arm supports a higher load range with less deflection. Each variant needs its own force-to-displacement calibration so readings remain meaningful across the intended range.

The scale follows the changing curvature of the pointer-tip path as the compliant arms bend, starting at 0 N in its unloaded home position. 04

Finite element analysis.

The original analysis files show the mesh, supports and applied load, followed by deformation studies at 10, 50 and 200 N. The studies show how the grip and indicator geometry respond as the load increases.

Grip-strength comparison: 200 N is the selected middle comparison value; the other references are 40 N and 800 N. For context on adult grip strength, see the international grip-strength study.

05

Results and next steps.

The supplied studies document a compliant load path and show increasing deformation across the three load cases. They support the design approach of using geometry to create indicator motion and tune sensitivity.

Geometry controls sensitivity.

The flexural arms provide the spring action. Width, length and thickness offer direct ways to change the force-to-displacement response while retaining the overall grip layout.

Material sets the usable range.

The report discusses PP copolymer and adopts an elastic modulus of 1.3 GPa and yield strength of 28 MPa for its material assessment. It proposes a static safety factor of at least 3.5 and a target stress of 8–10 MPa.

Calibrate the indicator.

Known-load measurements are needed to map pointer displacement to force and assess repeatability, hysteresis and recovery after unloading. These checks establish the usable scale for each material and stiffness variant.

The report also proposes a wider structural design target of at least 800 N. The supplied 10, 50 and 200 N images do not establish measured accuracy or confirm that target; calibration and structural assessment of the chosen material remain the next steps.
