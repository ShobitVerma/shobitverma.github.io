6-SPS Parallel Robot for Aseptic Remote Ultrasound

💡

Note: My contribution to this collaborative project was limited to mechanical design, development and assembly. The control electronics, circuit design and programming were handled by the wider project team.

Shobit Verma · Mechanical contribution with ADUZA Robotics & Automation.

01

A parallel robot for remote probe positioning.

The project explores a Stewart platform as a positioning mechanism for aseptic remote ultrasound. Six actuated legs connect a fixed structure to a moving platform, providing three translations and three rotations for the ultrasound-probe interface.

The application brings together a clinician’s motion input, a parallel mechanism and an end-effector attachment. My work supported the mechanical design and assembly of the platform: developing the physical layout, integrating actuator mounts and joints, and bringing the mechanism into a complete build.

Developed under ADUZA for the IIT Jodhpur–AIIMS Jodhpur remote-ultrasound application. ADUZA project profile ↗

02

From platform geometry to a buildable assembly.

The mechanical layout pairs six linear actuators between the fixed and moving structures. Joint locations, actuator travel and the relative sizes of the two platforms determine how the assembly can translate and tilt. Clearance around the legs and access to the end-effector interface are central to the design.

Layout.

Arrange the platform structure and leg attachment points around the required motion and probe interface.

Joint integration.

Connect the actuator ends while allowing the angular changes that accompany platform movement.

Assembly.

Bring the actuators, frames and attachment hardware together with clearance for the moving legs.

03

Integrating the physical system.

The complete build combines the six-leg mechanism with a supporting stand, a mobile base and a separate controller enclosure. The suspended configuration leaves the moving platform below the fixed structure, with the end-effector interface accessible from beneath.

Mechanical integration involves the alignment of the structure, actuator installation, joint freedom, cable clearance and access to fasteners. These interfaces connect the CAD design to the assembled robot.

04

Translation and orientation in six axes.

The six actuators work together to position and orient the moving platform. Equal or coordinated changes in leg length produce translations; differential changes produce roll, pitch and yaw. The usable motion depends on the actuator stroke, joint articulation and collision clearance.

05

How the wider system coordinates motion.

System context · Controls and electronics by the ADUZA project team.

The operator requests a platform pose through a joystick or a host application. Inverse kinematics converts that pose into six actuator-length targets. The controller drives the motor channels and uses actuator-position feedback to track those targets.

Joystick / host command→Pose & leg-length targets→Six motor channels→Platform motion

Actuator-position feedback returns to the controller.

The ADUZA demonstration describes a controller for DC-motor actuators with potentiometer feedback, with an API for custom applications and Python examples. The system models illustrate how commanded motion and feedback relate to the mechanism.
