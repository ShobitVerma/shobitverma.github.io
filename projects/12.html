Electronic Borehole Compass for Real-Time Borehole Trajectory Measurement and Borehole Survey

Contributors: Santu Kumar, Rabindra Kumar Sinha, Shobit Verma, Sikandar Kumar, Nishant Sinha, Sameer Sagar Sen.

01

See the path beneath the surface.

A drill hole can depart from its planned line as it passes through changing rock conditions. That deviation affects ore-body interpretation, blast-hole spacing and the point at which a hole reaches its target. This project develops a compact electronic compass to record borehole orientation and reconstruct the underground path.

The system brings together a machined probe, MEMS orientation sensors, embedded data logging and a mobile survey interface. The supplied presentation documents laboratory calibration at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad and field demonstrations at Moonidih Mines (BCCL) and Churcha Mines (SECL), alongside the report prepared for the upcoming paper.

02

From orientation to borehole position.

Inclination describes the angle between the borehole axis and the vertical. Azimuth describes its heading relative to north. Combining those angles with the measured distance along the hole allows successive survey stations to be placed in three-dimensional space.

Sense.

Measure gravity, angular motion and the local magnetic field using the probe’s accelerometer, gyroscope and magnetometer.

Correct.

Apply the probe’s calibration and tilt compensation to obtain stable orientation measurements.

Reconstruct.

Combine survey angles and depth intervals to calculate East, North and true vertical depth coordinates.

The report describes the Balanced Tangential Method for trajectory reconstruction. Each depth interval uses the directions at its two survey stations to estimate a displacement; accumulating those displacements builds the borehole path.

03

A compact probe built for deployment.

The cylindrical assembly houses the electronics and power supply behind a protective casing. Machined aluminium components, a nylon core and a spring at the front provide the mechanical structure and shock-absorption arrangement shown in the supplied design.

04

Sensors, firmware and local data storage.

The custom PCB connects the microcontroller, orientation sensors, regulated power and non-volatile storage. Firmware acquires sensor readings, computes orientation and logs time-stamped measurements. The presentation describes one-second recording of time, trend, plunge and azimuth to an SD card.

Acquire.

Sample the orientation sensors and pass their readings to the embedded controller.

Process.

Apply calibration, sensor fusion and spatial coordinate transformations to determine the probe’s orientation.

Store.

Retain survey records locally for later retrieval, processing and comparison with the planned drill path.

The report extends this workflow with Bluetooth communication and offline synchronisation. When a connection is unavailable downhole, the probe stores measurements onboard; the mobile application retrieves them after the probe reconnects.

05

A mobile survey workflow.

The report’s mobile application pairs with the probe, loads its calibration record and displays depth, azimuth, inclination, trend and plunge. Manual survey mode records a shot after a stabilisation interval; automatic logging collects repeated shots at the configured interval.

Start a survey.

Pair with the calibrated probe, create a project and enter the starting depth.

Record and review.

Capture survey stations, monitor the orientation readings and reconstruct the trajectory using the recorded depth intervals.

Sync and export.

Retrieve offline shots, keep the project on the mobile device and export the survey dataset to a spreadsheet.

The processing workflow applies hard-iron offsets and a 3 × 3 soft-iron correction matrix before tilt compensation. A gravity-vector check helps identify unstable readings. The report also describes local project storage and spreadsheet export for mine-planning records.

06

Laboratory calibration.

The presentation documents calibration of the developed compass against a total station in the Surveying Laboratory at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad. This provides a known orientation reference before field deployment. The supplied photographs show the calibration setup; the documents do not include a numerical table of angular accuracy or repeatability.

07

Field demonstrations at Moonidih and Churcha.

The supplied records show deployments at Moonidih Mines under BCCL and Churcha Mines under SECL. The report identifies the Moonidih team photograph as XV Seam, 7D/7L at a mine depth of 635 m; that is the test location’s depth, rather than a reported probe survey length.

Exploration.

Track borehole deviation to support interpretation of the intersection between a drill hole and an ore body.

Blast-hole surveying.

Record drill-hole orientation to help assess departure from a planned blasting pattern.

Fracture studies.

Use orientation records to support fracture delineation and in-situ stress investigation workflows.
