A personal project in kinetic art.

24-Bar Kinematic Mechanism with 1-DOF Dual-Phase Coupling for Gait Motion Synthesis

Designed by Shobit Verma.

01

Turning a gallop into a moving sculpture.

This personal art project explores how a cheetah’s running motion can be expressed through a mechanical linkage. The aim is a recognisable, flowing movement: the legs gather beneath the body, reach out and return through a repeated cycle, driven by one rotary input.

The work moves from six reference poses and traced paths to linkage synthesis, mirrored assemblies, gear-driven phase coupling and a CNC-cut prototype. The mechanism is a study in kinetic art, with motion and animal form developed together.

04

From reference paths to linkages.

The traced points provide a visual target for the linkage: the forward reach, low return path and lifted recovery need to belong to the same repeated motion. Front and rear legs are studied separately before their movements are brought into one assembly.

Observe.

Record the characteristic positions and the path followed by each selected reference point.

Synthesize.

Adjust the linkage geometry so its coupler path resembles the desired reach and recovery.

Coordinate.

Connect the leg mechanisms through a shared input and a repeatable phase relationship.

These figures show the visual path-matching process. Trajectory error and optimisation performance have not been quantified.

05

One input, mirrored motion.

The design uses a 24-bar mechanism using revolute joints and gears. Two similar linkage sets are arranged in a mirrored configuration and mechanically coupled with a phase difference, so they occupy different positions during the same input cycle. The gear train transfers motion while maintaining that relationship.

The repeated sequence combines lifting, forward motion and return. The phase difference coordinates the two sides from a single input, giving the sculpture its running-like rhythm without a separate drive for every leg.

06

The first CNC-cut prototype.

The first prototype translates the linkage plates into a physical assembly. The photographs show the cut components, their joints and the assembled cheetah form.

This build provides a basis for checking clearances, joint freedom and coordinated motion. It documents a moving prototype; locomotion speed, payload and endurance have not been measured. The speed labels in the animal-reference images describe the reference footage.
