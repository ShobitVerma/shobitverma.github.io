Robotic Load-Haul-Dumper (LHD) Scaled System for Mining Automation Technology Development

Inventors: Shobit Verma, Bryan Vas, Bhupendra Singh Rathore.

01

A research platform for underground loading and hauling.

An underground LHD loads broken rock, carries it through confined tunnels and dumps it at a designated point. This project brings that machine into a laboratory setting through a modular, centre-articulated prototype that supports mechanical development, hydraulic steering experiments and autonomy research.

The work combines a fabricated vehicle with a ROS-based navigation stack. AMCL localisation, A* path planning and model predictive control were demonstrated in a static-map simulation; applying that stack to the physical machine is part of the next development stage.

02

Mechanical design.

The chassis combines flat metal components suited to CNC machining with wedges and connectors that can be manufactured conventionally or by additive methods. The two chassis halves meet at a central articulation joint, while the front assembly carries the boom and bucket for the load, lift and dump cycle.

Modular chassis.

The complete model brings the two chassis sections, four-wheel running gear and raised bucket together in a compact laboratory platform.

Articulated steering.

Opposed hydraulic cylinders act across the central joint to change the angle between the chassis halves and guide the vehicle through a turn.

Bucket and boom.

The front assembly supports the bucket and its linkage, reproducing the load-handling arrangement needed to collect, raise and discharge material.

03

Hydraulic steering and instrumentation.

The steering study connects a pump and relief valve to the articulated steering cylinder through an Orbitrol valve driven by a stepper motor, with a proportional directional control valve shown as a second actuation arrangement. Pressure, flow and rod-position measurements provide feedback; the separate loading circuit in the drawing is a test rig that applies resistance to the steering actuator.

Command.

The controller drives the steering-valve input to meter hydraulic flow towards the commanded articulation state.

Actuate.

The hydraulic cylinder changes its length and turns the two chassis halves about their central joint.

Measure.

Rod position, cylinder pressures and flows describe actuator motion and the load applied during the steering experiment.

04

Localise, plan and follow.

The navigation architecture separates the LHD client, LHD server and path-planning service. The client validates a target and requests a path; the server tracks the reference trajectory while returning feedback. This structure keeps target selection, route generation and vehicle control as distinct parts of the same navigation loop.

AMCL localisation.

Laser readings and odometry update the estimated vehicle pose against a known two-dimensional occupancy map.

A* planning.

The planner searches an inflated costmap from the current pose to a validated goal and reconstructs a traversable reference path.

MPC tracking.

The vehicle model predicts future motion, and a CasADi optimiser selects a constrained input sequence to reduce trajectory error.

05

The physical build and navigation demonstration.

The fabricated prototype shows the rear chassis, front chassis, bucket, steering valve and articulation cylinder as a complete vehicle. The navigation evidence demonstrates localisation and trajectory following after a goal is set in simulation, providing a basis for integrating the autonomy software with the machine.

The next steps are a scaled mine environment, integration of the controller with the physical prototype, and navigation extensions for dynamic obstacles and behaviour trees. The results do not establish autonomous bucket loading or on-machine autonomous navigation.
