01

A cutting point is only the beginning.

Vision can locate a tomato pedicel approximately, but the cutter still needs to capture it and bring it into the blade region. I designed a kinematically coupled hook that moves with the scissor arms, pulling the pedicel into place before the blades finish closing. The hook and cutter share a single input rather than needing separate actuators.

I developed and refined the mechanism through more than 20 prototypes, investigated its behaviour in field tests, and derived the hook trajectory using 40 defined parameters and 20 linear and trigonometric equations. The result connects physical linkage geometry to a predictable hooking, pulling and holding sequence.

05

TESTING WITH REAL TOMATOES

Field tests.

What the plants revealed.

I tested the mechanism on a harvesting setup and checked the interaction between the hook, stem and blades. The early tests identified weak pulling and looseness after repeated operation, making hook strength, joint fit and the available cutting region central to the next iterations.

The later six-bar design reduced linkage complexity and focused on stronger hooking, a wider cutting region and a more stable holding position.

06

CROSS-CHECKING THE MODEL

Governing equation validation by comparing MATLAB results with CAD geometry.

I evaluated the governing equations in MATLAB and compared the output angle and hook coordinates with a kinematically constrained SolidWorks sketch. The tabulated results at three input angles agree at the displayed numerical precision.

SIX LINKS, ONE INPUT

The hook follows the scissor.

Closing the two main arms drives the initiation, supporting and handling bars. Their linked rotations guide the hook towards the cutting region while the blades close. The shape and proportions of the links set the trajectory mechanically.

01 & 02

Main arms

Carry the two cutting blades and receive the closing input.

03

Initiation bar

Transfers arm movement into the grappling linkage.

04

Supporting bar

Rotates about its pivot and drives the handling bar.

05

Handling bar

Guides the hook inward and controls the transition between phases.

06

Curved hook

Captures the pedicel, draws it towards the blades and stabilizes it.
